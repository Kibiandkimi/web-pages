Walk, cycleor shuttle?

The pros and cons of three ways to get to class — measured, not guessed.

Walk Bike Campus shuttle

English class presentation

Hello, everyone. Every day on this campus we make the same small decision: do I walk, take a bike, or wait for the shuttle bus?

Most of us decide by habit. We decided to measure instead. I will show the real advantages and disadvantages of each choice, and finish with advice for students and for the university.

But the bike has weaknesses. First, hills. On a five percent slope, a cyclist needs about thirty percent more time; a walker, only five.

Second, steps. You get off and push, so steps cost a cyclist six times the normal time.

Third, the class bell. On crowded shared paths, a bike takes a third longer, while walkers do not slow down at all.

Under about three hundred metres, the bike saves less than two minutes. And you still have to unlock and park it.

So, side by side. A typical trip from dorm to class takes thirteen minutes on foot, five by bike, and sixteen by shuttle, wait included.

Walking is the most reliable: the same time, every time. But it is slow: nearly three times the bike's time.

The bike is the fastest: it wins on all twenty-four routes. But it suffers from hills, steps, crowds and parking.

The shuttle is the easiest way up the hill. But the stops are far and the wait is unknown, so it beats walking on only three routes.

On foot or by bike, add 20 %. That margin covered 86 % of our recorded trips.

So what should a student do? Three simple rules.

One: under three hundred metres, walk.

Two: for anything further, cycle, even over the hill. From Huiwen to Zhili, it is nineteen minutes on foot and six by bike.

Three: take the shuttle only if a stop is near both doors, for example from Qiao or Haishan to Huiwen. Even on the best route, it is worth waiting ten minutes at most.

And on foot or by bike, add twenty percent to your estimate. Of course, in heavy rain, comfort matters more than minutes.

And the university? Three fixes.

One: show the wait. Today, not every bus has a tracker. Put one on every bus, and a countdown at every stop. The longest gap was more than twenty minutes.

Two: bring the bus closer. South Dorms are a nine-minute walk from the nearest useful stop: give them one. Extra runs up to Huiwen at class change would help too.

Three: clear the way for bikes. Build ramps beside the steps, and add bike lanes and parking at the doors.

In one breath

Walk the short ones. Cycle the long ones. Wait only if the stop is at your door.

Thank you — questions are welcome.

To finish, the whole talk in one breath. Walk the short ones. Cycle the long ones. And wait for the shuttle only if the stop is at your door.

So tomorrow, before you join the queue at the bus stop, ask yourself: would walking be faster?
