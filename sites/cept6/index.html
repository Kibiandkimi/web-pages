The belief

NOT QUITE

But the bike is not perfect. First, hills. On a five percent slope, a cyclist needs about thirty percent more time. A walker needs only five.

Second, steps. You get off and push, so steps cost a cyclist six times the normal time.

Third, the class bell. On crowded shared paths, a bike takes a third longer, while walkers do not slow down at all.

And on very short trips, the bike hardly saves any time.

So, is cycling never slowed down? Not quite.

On foot or by bike, allow 20% extra time. That margin covered 86% of our recorded trips.

For students, we have three simple rules.

One: under three hundred metres, walk. The bike saves less than two minutes.

Two: for anything further, cycle, even over the hill. Remember the test: up the hill to Huiwen, eighteen minutes on foot and six by bike.

Three: take the shuttle only if a stop is near both doors, for example from Qiao or Haishan to Huiwen. Even on the best route, the bus must come within ten minutes, or walking wins.

And on foot or by bike, allow twenty percent extra time.

And the university? We suggest three fixes. One: show the wait, with a tracker on every bus and a countdown at every stop.

Two: bring the bus closer. South Dorms are a nine-minute walk from the nearest useful stop.

Three: clear the way for bikes, with ramps beside the steps.

In one breath

Walk the short ones. Cycle the long ones. Wait only if the stop is at your door.

To finish, our whole talk in one breath. Walk the short ones. Cycle the long ones. And wait for the shuttle only if the stop is at your door.

So tomorrow morning, before you join the queue at the shuttle stop, ask yourself: would walking be faster?
