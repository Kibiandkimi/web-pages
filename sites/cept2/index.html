Good morning everyone. Every day on this campus we make the same small decision: do I walk, do I ride a bike, or do I wait for the shuttle bus? I recorded my own GPS tracks to find out how these options really compare. The moving lines behind me are simulated walkers in blue and cyclists in orange, each moving at the speed my model predicts for that road.

1 · Wikipedia (zh), “深圳大学”, section Transport.

There are three options. Walking is free and goes everywhere, but it's slow. Riding is fast, but hills, steps and crowds get in the way. And the shuttle bus needs no effort, but you have to wait for it. These three exist for a reason: our campus is hilly, and when the university banned electric motorbikes in 2012, it introduced shuttles and shared bikes instead. I have 12 tracks on foot and 13 by bike. For the shuttle I have no data yet, so you'll see it as a dashed "ghost" throughout the talk.

03 · The terrain

The campus is a hill

Huiwen sits at 33 m. The southern teaching buildings sit at 5–8 m. Every trip between them climbs or drops about 25 m.

Terrain from the DirMap shared height model (10 m grid), drawn in 2 m layers with height exaggerated.

Before comparing the modes, one thing about our campus: it's a hill. Huiwen in the north is at about 33 metres. The southern teaching buildings, Zhili, Zhizhen and Zhixin, are at only 5 to 8 metres. So any trip between north and south climbs or drops about 25 metres. Keep this in mind, because it affects walking and cycling very differently.

Switching sides of campus?

Huiwen to the south cluster takes 20–25 min on foot, 6–8 min by bike. Ride.

Our breaks are 5, 10 or 20 minutes, and you need about a minute to leave one building and enter another. On foot, only one of the fifteen pairs of teaching buildings fits a five-minute break, and five pairs don't even fit the twenty-minute break: anything between Huiwen and the southern cluster. By bike, every single pair fits a ten-minute break.

09 · Hills & steps

Hills and steps punish the bike, not the walker

Riding up to Huiwen or Sci-Tech takes 15–19% longer than riding down, about one minute. On foot the difference is under 2%.

Here's why direction and terrain matter. On the left: a 5 percent uphill makes a bike about 24 percent slower, but a walker only about 4 percent. On the right: special paths. Footbridges double a cyclist's time, and steps mean pushing the bike, about six times slower. A walker barely notices footbridges, and steps cost only about twice the time. So riding up to Huiwen takes about a minute longer than riding down; walking is nearly the same both ways.

Plan a buffer

Add about 10% to a walk and 25% to a ride. Most of the cycling error is how fast you happen to ride that day.

Two more differences. At class change, cyclists on shared footways take about 26 percent longer because of the crowds; walkers show no measurable slowdown. And predictability: I hid each track from the model and predicted it. Walking predictions were off by about 10 percent; cycling by about 23. So when you plan, add about 10 percent to a walk and a quarter to a ride.

12 · Verdict

Pros and cons at a glance

measuredexpected, not yet measured?unknown until we have data
Putting it together. Walking wins on hills, rush hour and predictability. Riding wins on speed, reach and fitting short breaks, but it suffers on hills, steps and crowded paths. The shuttle should win on effort and weather, and it doesn't care about the hill, but its speed, its crowding and above all its waiting time are still question marks.
13 · Advice for students

Walk short. Ride long. Wait smart.

Walk · under ~300 m, or next door

The bike saves under 2 minutes, which unlocking and parking eat. Zhili ⇄ Zhizhen: 3.4 min on foot.

Ride · crossing the hill, or over ~1 km

Huiwen → Zhili: 20.0 min on foot, 5.7 min by bike. Going up? Leave a minute early.

Wait · rain, heat, heavy bags, or an injury

Long uphill trips, when the shuttle is close. Check the wait first: past ~10 minutes, walking wins.

Try it
And four habits
  • At class change, ride on roads, not footways (×1.26).
  • Avoid steps and footbridges on a bike: push = ×6.5 the time.
  • Pad a ride by 25%, a walk by 10%.
  • Short break, far building: ride or sit near the door.
So my advice fits in six words: walk short, ride long, wait smart. Walk anything under about 300 metres or next door. Ride when you cross the hill or go further than a kilometre, and leave a minute early when you're going up. Take the shuttle when it rains, when it's hot, when you carry something heavy, or when you're injured, but check the wait first. On the right you can see a few examples, and four small habits that save time.

I also have four suggestions for the campus. First, ramps next to steps: there are about fifty flights of steps on the map, and each one forces cyclists to push or detour. Second, bike parking right at the building doors, so that short rides are worth it. Third, a shuttle that runs the climb, north to south, timed to our breaks, with live arrival times so nobody waits blindly. And fourth, bike lanes on the busiest shared paths, where cyclists lose a quarter of their time at the bell.
15 · Limitations & next steps

  • Places are building centroids: real doors add tens of seconds.
  • No shuttle trips yet: walk to stop, waiting time, ride time, crowding.
